## Independent oracle design and coverage

Ten declared shapes were exercised: 1×1, 1×4, 4×1, 2×2, 2×4, 4×2, 3×4, 4×4, 3×5, and 4×5. **Every coefficient position was used as the kernel origin**, giving **92 origin cases**. The 4×5 kernel contains a single off-centre delta and zero border coefficients, so its declared support remains fully exercised despite sparse optical support. The remaining shapes include asymmetric, even, horizontal and vertical kernels. The explicit 2×4 weights were `[1 2 5 11; 7 3 13 22]/64`.

Each origin was checked with a shared kernel and a three-plane bank with distinct flipped/reordered kernels (**184 bank cases**), and a repeated explicit bank was required to match the shared kernel exactly. Association used wavelengths `[430;550;690]` nm. The convolution oracle gathers source values by their physical integer displacement; it does not call `conv2`. Physical edge arrays, retained original indices and all four required halo lengths were asserted exactly. Another **92 cases** used the smallest input equal to the declared support and checked the single remaining cell and its physical coordinates.

The principal transfer oracle uses zero-padded `fft2` plus an explicitly derived origin phase. It evaluates 17×17 signed frequencies including both Nyquist endpoints, then compares the full complex OTF, not only magnitude. A second oracle factors the direct DFT into row/column exponential matrix products at non-FFT signed frequencies. Origin phase changes, origin-invariant magnitude, DC, Hermitian symmetry, and the nonnegative-kernel magnitude bound were checked separately. Numerical tolerances scale with coefficient count and binary64 epsilon. No oracle calls the existing optics test helpers.

**276 interior shifted impulses** independently verify orientation, translation, complete-support mass and physical first moments. Every origin also receives three exact cell-average oblique sinusoidal spectral planes. Predicted optical fields use the FFT transfer and the returned physical cell centres, so correlation reversal, swapped x/y axes, an opposite phase sign, or a crop with incorrect coordinates changes the result.

**16 complete spatial pipelines** use all eight origins of the asymmetric 2×4 kernel and sensor pitch equal to one or two input cells. The inputs are exact continuous-sinusoid cell averages; the expected output has the analytical continuous square-pixel average at the target pitch and the declared discrete PSF transfer. Expected charge is calculated independently with pixel area, fill factor, QE, bin width and exposure once. The optical-only OTF must be byte-identical whether or not an optional aperture reference is requested. Seven deliberate spectral-association, Nyquist, nonuniform-grid and support errors were rejected with the expected identifiers.

A final bounded 63×63 kernel check covers four corners and the central origin, direct DFT agreement, DC/magnitude bounds and constant fields at 1e-300 and 1e300. It does not sweep all 3,969 origins of that maximum-size kernel.

## Aperture accounting conclusion

No double application of square-aperture averaging was observed. The 16 physical pipelines matched the target-cell analytical average to `1.2336509379220724e-15` relative error, while a deliberate second aperture factor differed by at least `0.0092293510921144118` relative (about 0.923%). This is a sensitive wrong-model control. `opticalMtf` retains the optical transfer unchanged and reports the continuous square-aperture product separately, consistent with the design caveat.

## Normalization / MTF bound qualification

The constructor intentionally allows coefficient summation roundoff, with tolerance `32 * coefficient_count * eps(1)`, and does not renormalize. Therefore an admitted kernel can have DC and MTF slightly greater than one by that accepted mass error. A 63×63 declared kernel with one nonzero coefficient `1 + 16 * 3969 * eps(1)` was accepted with mass excess `1.4100720591159188e-11`; its DC excess was exactly the same value. This is documented tolerance behavior, **not a defect finding**, and establishes why a strict `MTF <= 1` assertion would be inappropriate. Ordinary normalized small-kernel cases exceeded one by at most one epsilon; the largest ordinary 63×63 DC error was `2.886579864025407e-15`.
