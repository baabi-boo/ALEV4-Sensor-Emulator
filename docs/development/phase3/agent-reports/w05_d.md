## Project reconstruction and reference review

`isetPointGrid.m` requires all interior native x/y knots to be present in output edges (29–31), requires complete point-support coverage (26–27), and obtains reconstructed corner values with bilinear interpolation (50). Every output rectangle therefore remains within a single bilinear patch. Its four-corner mean (65–67) is the exact rectangular mean for that declared field. It retains density units; no pixel area or exposure is introduced at this conversion.

`isetSpatialFixture.m` includes both optical knots and all sensor edges in the exact partition (139–144). Thus the conservative overlap operation consumes whole or correctly aligned subdivisions of the declared field before spectral integration. Its coarser refinement series deliberately omits the full sensor-edge partition and improves the piecewise-constant cell approximation with factors `[1 2 4 8 16]`. The coarse controls are informative: the checker is measurably nonuniform, and both native center-only sampling and coarse source averaging produce substantial error.

The reference in `isetSpatialFixture.m`, 271–313, first integrates effective QE and spectral width at native optical points. It then integrates each pixel's correct Bayer channel over scalar rectangles split at native knots. Its scalar bilinear evaluator does not call `interp2`, `isetPointGrid`, `cfaMap`, or `resampleSensorIrradiance`. Spectral integration commutes with the linear spatial reconstruction, so this different operation order is physically matched. The reference's rectangle area supplies the collecting area; only fill factor and exposure are multiplied afterward (300).

The native dimensional chain is:

`photon density × QE × wavelength width × electron charge × spatial area × exposure × conversion gain / electron charge / conversion gain = electrons`.

The project chain averages density spatially and then multiplies effective QE, bin width, pixel area, fill factor, and exposure exactly once. These are the same expectation within the restricted full-fill model.

`simulateSpatialExposure.m`, 53–61, makes post-optics input an explicit bypass: `psf=[]`, optical/input arrays identical, and operation order begins with area averaging. A supplied nonempty PSF is rejected. The executed fixture confirmed that exact behavior; neither the project wrapper nor the 1×1 native prefilter adds a second optical blur.
